the little man

in the toy shop window sat a tiny gingerbread man. he gave them a cookie too.

then he was in the bookshop window. and the shoe shop’s. each time he asked for it, and hansel dug it out.

gretel noticed. it was always the same little man.

— 5 —

cookies
everywhere

soon every shop gave them one. the shoe shop remembered her size. the bookshop remembered she liked dragons.

each cookie only ever went back to the shop that baked it.

the basket got heavier.

(hansel’s arms noticed.)

— 4 —

the first cookie

the shopkeeper had an idea. “may i give you a cookie?” “yes please.” it had GRETEL iced on it. hansel tucked it in the basket.

next morning he held it up. “gretel! the red kite again?”

it worked.

— 3 —

once upon a time…

in the village by the woods, every shop forgot you the minute you walked out.

gretel did the talking. hansel did the carrying. (he kept everything in tins. buttons. beetles. other tins.)

gretel wished, just once, someone would remember her.

— 2 —

the witch’s house

they followed the little man home, deep into the woods. he whispered to the witch, and she wrote it all down.

she smiled. “hello, dears. toy shop at nine. bookshop at ten. the dragon book, twice.”

she knew everywhere they’d been.

— 6 —

one tin for every shop

gretel looked at the list, then at hansel’s tins. “one tin for every shop.” hansel sorted.

and the little man’s cookie? gretel ate it. now the little man in each window got a different cookie.

the witch’s list was full of strangers.

— 7 —

the moral

a cookie remembers you. ask who’s doing the remembering.

on the real web, the little men are ads and buttons found on lots of sites. many browsers now keep one tin per site, just like hansel.

every page has windows. who’s sitting in them?

(the toy shop still remembers the red kite.)
