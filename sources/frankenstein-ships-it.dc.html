the villagers arrive

no logs. no alerts. no idea what's happening inside. prod is the lab.

so when something breaks, the villagers (sorry, users) show up with pitchforks. and torches. and screenshots. in comic sans.

nobody meant to cause harm. it shows up anyway.

IT'S ALIVE!

tests? skipped. docs? none. the sticky note that said “maybe don't”? lost.

he ships it. alone. on a friday.

it runs. barely. but it runs.

— 4 —

the parts bin

the creature is stitched together from whatever was lying around:

• a vendor module, copy-pasted
• a dependency that might be cursed
• a service older than the intern
• open-source code from 2016: maintained by nobody, feared by all

— 3 —

a dark & stormy sprint

a brilliant engineer works alone, late at night, in a candlelit lab.

dr. frankenstein means well. he's ambitious. he wants to make life better.

he's also surrounded by spare parts and questionable ideas, like: “what if i give it two brains?”

— 2 —

the real mistake

building the creature wasn't the worst part. abandoning it was. the doctor vanished right after go-live.

the creature isn't evil. when it starts breaking things, that's not a bug. it's a cry for help.

you didn't patch it. you ghosted it.

— 6 —

take two

this time, raise it right:

• think up how it could go wrong, before the lightning
• test it, then test it weirder
• give it logs, so it can tell you where it hurts
• keep a name on the on-call list

— 7 —

the moral

don't just ship something alive. ship something you'll care for.

build things you'd be proud to own at 3 a.m., when the phone rings and your only thought is “oh no. what did i do?”

you don't have to be perfect. you just have to stick around.

(the creature would like that.)
