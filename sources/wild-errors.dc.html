the 403

Codus prohibitus

guards the gate. its cousin the 401 asks “who are you?” the 403 knows exactly who you are. the answer is still no.

habitat: admin pages, the cookie jar

catch difficulty: don’t. it’s doing its job.

the 404

Codus absens

you’ve seen its sign a hundred times. but go looking for the page and you only find the spot where it should be. (it isn’t hiding. there’s just nothing there.)

habitat: links from 2009, typos

catch difficulty: ☆☆☆☆☆ (nothing to catch)

— 4 —

the 301

Codus migratorius

flew away and never came back. but it’s polite: it leaves a note saying where it went, and your browser follows without a word.

habitat: old addresses, sites that moved to https

catch difficulty: ★☆☆☆☆
(it tells you where it went)

— 3 —

how to identify

every one of them carries a number. look at the first digit: it tells you the family.

2 happy. it worked.
3 migratory. it moved.
4 the problem’s on your side.
5 the server tripped.

(1xx are the larvae. you’ll almost never see one.)

— 2 —

the 503

Codus hibernans

too tired, too busy, or out for maintenance. leave it alone and it’s usually back by morning. (poking it makes it grumpier.)

habitat: launch days, the minute tickets go on sale

catch difficulty: ★★☆☆☆
(just wait)

— 6 —

field notes: the rarest one

april 1, 1998. someone wrote a serious-looking standard for brewing coffee over the internet. ask a teapot for coffee and it answers:

418, i’m a teapot.

in 2017, engineers tried to remove it. people protested until it stayed. today it’s officially reserved: a protected species.
