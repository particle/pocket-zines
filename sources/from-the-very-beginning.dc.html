the long way

“the frame doesn't know 03 means 2003,” said the grandkid, and wrote every year the long way: 1946, 2003.

baby grandma moved up, almost to the front. then the doorbell rang.

no time to check again.

— 5 —

the dog grows young

“put the biggest thing first,” said the grandkid. “the year!”

now the old dog (03-10-9) came first, and the puppy (90-3-15) came last.

the old dog turned into a puppy.

(lucky dog.)

— 4 —

letter by letter

that night, the grandkid watched it alone. the frame read each file name one letter at a time, left to right, and never wondered what they meant.

the wedding (4-20-74) came before baby grandma (6-12-46), because 4 comes before 6.

grandma got married before she was born.

— 3 —

once upon a time…

grandma was turning 80, and it was a surprise.

the grandkid scanned a shoebox of old photos for a slideshow, to show grandma her whole life, from the very beginning.

the frame would put them in order. sort of.

— 2 —

surprise!

the lights went down. first slide: a baby in a santa hat. second slide: that same baby, being born.

(1946-12-25 beat 1946-6-12. 1 comes before 6.) “she always said she was ahead of her time,” said grandpa. everyone laughed, even grandma. the grandkid didn't.

this was not the beginning.

— 6 —

a few little zeros

the grandkid slipped over and gave every month and day two digits: 1946-06-12.

the slideshow started again. baby grandma. her first christmas. the wedding. mom. the puppy. the old dog. the grandkid.

grandma cried. the good kind.

— 7 —

the moral

biggest first, and everything falls into place.

write dates year-month-day, with the whole year and two digits for the rest. anything that reads left to right sorts them right: you, a photo frame, a folder on your computer.

try it: name your next file 2026-10-30-something, then sort the folder.

(grandpa still says she's ahead of her time.)
